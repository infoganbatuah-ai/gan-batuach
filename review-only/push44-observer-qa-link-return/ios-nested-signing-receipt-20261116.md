# Observer QA iOS nested-signing correction (2026-11-16)

The first locally signed `com.digitalobserver.app.qa` build installed on the physical iPhone 13 Pro but terminated immediately. The bounded device console identified `App.debug.dylib` as unsigned. After signing that dylib and the outer app, local launch and reopen succeeded; the closed QA hostname still did not resolve, as expected. A stricter source-level audit then found another unsigned nested binary, `__preview.dylib`, which was not loaded during that test. Outer `codesign --verify --deep --strict` alone did not detect either missing nested signature in this build.

`scripts/qa/sign-observer-ios-qa.mjs` is the controlled local recipe. It requires the exact Observer QA bundle ID, an explicit Apple Development identity SHA-1 and QA entitlements without `aps-environment`. It signs nested dylibs/frameworks in depth order, verifies each nested signature, signs the app last, and re-verifies the complete artifact. Its `verify` mode checks nested code independently and fails on an unsigned dylib. This script does not build, install, launch, upload or activate an endpoint.

Focused regression compiles an isolated local dylib, removes its signature, and proves `verify` fails closed. The recipe also ran successfully against a copy of the actual signed QA artifact: four nested code items passed verification and the resulting app had no Push entitlement. The resulting local archive is `.dev-cache/tmp/ObserverQA-auth-all-nested-signed-20261116.zip` in the isolated worktree, SHA-256 `0ddc1c7304785fd5a09e50c2c998b49c66a9a80617537ba088787f6d03d3e840`, 17,929,737 bytes. The all-nested-signed archive was subsequently installed on the approved iPhone 13 Pro. CoreDevice reported a successful launch and a second launch after terminate-existing, with no dyld/signing crash. The closed QA hostname did not resolve; no authenticated Product or external-route proof is claimed.

The source change is limited to this QA signing script, focused test, and receipt. Integration into the canonical Development branch requires normal review and CI by the owning thread. Future signing must use this nested-first recipe or an equivalent Xcode export that independently verifies every nested executable; the earlier archive must not be used as a launchable qualification artifact.
